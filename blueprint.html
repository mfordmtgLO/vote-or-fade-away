02

Design spotlight: The Fade sees his next opponent

The one rule that shapes the entire pre-game pipeline.

Core design rule · Author's law Live — in the shipped game

The fighting order is fixed in advance — because he already read it.

The Fade is a disinformation villain who fights with excuses, spin, and misinformation — never with a sword. In the browser game he has 100 HP (break him to 40 to win); at the tabletop he's harder, at 185 HP. Every claim he makes is answered the same way:

His claim→The trick, named→Documented fact→What it proves

Mid-battle he deploys four minions — Disinfo Imp, Tariff Wraith, Rate-Freeze Golem, Spiral Specter (built in literal form from the brief's villains: red tape, tariff price tags, oil barrels, a chained ballot box) — each banished by picking the right correction. The fifth figure answers to no one: the Golden Parachute, an independent opportunist floated in from the real world — a captain paid millions as his ship went down — who drops in on his own trigger, the first time Mission Creep patches The Fade at 60 HP or below, happy to keep the war, and his payday, going.

Why the opener must be pre-chosen: because The Fade always sees his next opponent, his opening taunts aren't generic. They're written against the specific hero on deck — their class, their backstory, their likely excuse for staying home. Lock the band without naming who goes first, and his best material has no target. So when a band of 3, 4, or 6 is locked, a designated opener is locked with it — and Kendo loads that order into Roll20 before the session starts.

Try it — pick the opener

See what foresight buys him. The taunt re-aims at whoever you put on deck.

The Fade, on Maya Reyes

“Ah, the Ranger goes first. Of course. Rangers always think the trail marks itself — I've already moved the signs, Maya. Tell me again why your one walk to the polls changes the map?” — a jab built from her class and her tales, answered only by a documented fact.

Illustrative of the mechanism: in play, the taunt and the misinformation claim are keyed to the locked opener's sheet and backstory — specific, personal, and wrong on purpose, so the correction lands.

Status, honestly: the opener pre-selection mechanic (spec dated 2026-10-30) is built and live in the shipped game; the picker on this page mirrors the in-game step — pick an opener and The Fade's taunt re-aims at that hero.

The Forge — character sheet designer

A shared web app where each friend forges their own character on the same sheet engine as the six heroes. The app is built and verified code-side; its Firebase backend setup is a parked owner sitting, so it isn't live for the group yet.

  • StackVanilla JS + Firebase Auth / Firestore / Storage, Google sign-in, no build step

  • Into the gameForged characters import into Power Card: a queue of up to 20, swapped into a battle-ready band of 6 — a 26-character total roster

Roll20 bridge — ForgeImporter

Roll20 has no public API. Its “API” is a sealed Mod-script sandbox — JavaScript inside the campaign, no outbound web calls, no external libraries. So the bridge meets Roll20 on its own terms: a chat command.

  • HowA Forge sheet exports as one copyable line — !forge <base64url JSON> — pasted into Roll20 chat; the script parses it and creates the full character: stats, derived modifiers, weapons, bio
  • LimitsPortraits can't be pushed by script — they upload through the Roll20 web UI (Kendo does this in the pre-game load)
  • StatusImporter written and simulation-tested (valid payloads build characters; garbled payloads are rejected cleanly). Not yet installed — a quiet end-to-end test comes before any game night

Backstory integration — one seed, every surface

A single canonical BACKSTORY.md in the game repo is the seed asset: the night the game was built, the design laws, the war economy, the thesis. Every hero's three Campaigns of Friends scrolling tales end with the same vote-weave; forged characters get tales written in the same voice. That's what lets the band persist — recognizably the same people — across the browser game, the tabletop module, the Forge, and Roll20, instead of being re-invented on each surface.

Pre-game pipeline — push / pull

How a character goes from a friend's idea to battle-ready before anyone says “roll initiative.”

▲ Push — toward game night

1

Players push their sheetsEach player picks the character from their own Forge stable best equipped for the night's module and pushes the sheet to Mike.

2

Kendo generates, pushes backPortrait sets are drawn in batches (9 files per character) and pushed back into the players' own sheets — attires, masks, tales.

3

The band is pre-selected — with an openerThe night's band of 3, 4, or 6 is locked, fighting order fixed, and a designated opener named — because The Fade's taunts are written against whoever goes first (see §02).

4

Kendo pre-loads Roll20The whole band — sheets, stats, portraits — is built in the campaign before any player joins. Nobody watches setup; they arrive at a ready table.

▼ Pull — back into the game

1

Game import pulls Forge characters inPower Card pulls a player's completed Forge characters into its import carousel — from their own account, owner-scoped. Nobody pulls anyone else's heroes.

2

Queue of 20, band of 6Up to 20 imports wait in the queue; the player swaps them into a battle-ready band of 6 alongside the original heroes — 26 roster spots in total.

3

Same sheet, same voiceBecause the Forge and the game share one sheet engine and one backstory canon, a pulled character arrives with stats, look, and tales intact — not a re-roll.

06

Session automation — Mike + Kendo

Built · Not yet live

The Campaigns ledger lives in the Forge, admin-only. It's the game-night record and the launchpad in one: who played, as whom, in what — and the one prompt that builds the table.

  1. Days before

    The group picks the module in a group text — the example of record: “Module Q1 — Queen of the Demonweb Pits.” Each player pushes their chosen sheet to Mike (pipeline §05).

  2. Mike confirms

    Mike names the night and confirms the roster. Each character's owner is pre-connected to the sheet; Mike can override the display name for the night — real name or D&D pseudo-name, per campaign. Names can differ night to night.

  3. One prompt

    The PLAY button builds a setup prompt: campaign + date + per-player human name, character, and Roll20 import blob. Mike pastes that one prompt to Kendo.

  4. Kendo loads

    Kendo signs into Roll20 as Mike (the GM), installs the ForgeImporter script, pre-builds the whole band, uploads the portraits the script can't push, and reports READY in chat.

  5. Game night

    The group joins on Discord audio and plays. Across-the-pond group — Oregon to the U.K. — arriving at a table that's already set, opener already on deck, The Fade already talking.

  6. Next day

    The record closes the loop: the campaign is saved (searchable by human name, date, date range, or character), Mike adds highlights notes, and a draft-email follow-up — assembled from the record, opened as a mailto draft in his own mail program — goes out the next day.
