How the Forge Works  ·  Explaining the Power Card Character Forge

How the Forge Works

From a blank sheet to a hero with portraits, a past, and a place in the band — the whole path a character travels through the Power Card Character Forge, into the game, and onto the table.

The Forge is a character-sheet designer built for the D&D group behind Power Card — up to thirty friends, from Oregon to the U.K. It runs on the same sheet engine as the game’s six packaged heroes, so a character made here is not an imitation of a game hero. It is built the same way, on the same sheet, from the first slider to the last tale.

The flow — five steps

  1. 1

    Build your hero

    A player builds a character on the same sheet engine as the game’s six packaged heroes:

    Name Class Point-buy attribute sliders Gear picks — weapon, spells, pack A Look — skin tone and hair color
  2. 2

    Send it to Mike

    One tap: “Send to Mike to create” — in the app, submit to Kendo for sketch creation. There is no account wrangling for friends beyond a Google sign-in, and each player’s characters are their own.

  3. 3

    The set comes back

    The finished character returns as a complete set: three finished portraits — three attires, in the game’s pencil-sketch style — the Look variants that let the sheet re-dress the portrait, and three backstory tales written for that character (the spotlight below). The set attaches to the player’s own sheet in the Forge.

    Portraits arrive as a matched set, and a character is only marked complete when the full set — portraits, variants, and tales — is in.

  4. 4

    Import into the game

    Finished heroes import straight into Power Card itself. They join the import queue, take a roster spot beside the packaged six, and can be picked for the battle-ready band — of 3, 4, or 6 — exactly like a packaged hero.

  5. 5

    Play it at the table

    The same sheet exports to Roll20 — “Copy for Roll20.” On game night, the group’s picks are recorded in a Campaigns entry, and Mike + Kendo pre-load the band into Roll20 before anyone joins on Discord audio.

Three tries, then the Stable

Your character sheet includes a character portrait, and that portrait starts from your own submitted prompt description — Kendo creates the first portrait from that description.

Before you finalize the character, you get a total of 3 design prompt requests to have Kendo further customize the portrait: the initial prompt, plus 2 more.

Those rounds are not portrait-only: on every round, Kendo also revises the character’s three backstory tales, rewritten from everything you have submitted so far — your original description plus each round’s added detail — so the portrait and the scrolling backstories come back together and grow with the character. The third round’s tales are final, and they lock when the sheet is Saved to the Stable.

After the 3rd customization

When the 3rd customization comes back, an alert says that was the final design change. You then choose: delete the character, or move it to the Stable.

“Save to Stable” means moving the finished design into a finalized character sheet in one of the Stable’s open slots. The Stable is your own roster of finished characters, and it holds a maximum of 20 characters.

If the Stable is already full — all 20 slots taken — when you try to save, a popup appears:

Popup · Stable full

“Remove a character sheet from the Stable to free up a slot.”

The spotlight

Your hero arrives with a past

A forged character does not show up as a blank sheet. It comes back with three backstory tales of its own, instantly created for that character as part of its returned set — one tale per signature move, each ending with the same vote weave that closes the packaged heroes’ tales.

In the game, those tales live under “Campaigns of Friends” on the hero’s sheet, on the same animated move tiles as the six packaged heroes. Tap a tile and your hero’s tale opens in the shared tale panel and scrolls up slowly, on its own — exactly like Maya’s, Dre’s, and Priya’s do. Tap the panel to pause or resume.

A forged hero is not a guest in the story — it ships with legends of its own, in the same form, on the same shelf.

Campaigns of Friends · Tale of the first move Scrolling…

This is where your hero’s tale plays. The moment the finished set comes back, the tale is already written — created for this character, for this move — and it waits on the hero’s sheet under Campaigns of Friends, on the same animated tiles the packaged heroes use.

Tap the tile, and the tale opens here, in the shared panel. Then it begins to rise — slowly, on its own — line after line, the way Maya’s, Dre’s, and Priya’s tales rise when their tiles are tapped. No blank page. No placeholder. A past, ready to be read.

And like every packaged hero’s tale, it closes the same way: with the vote weave — the reminder woven into the end of the story that a voice only counts when it is cast, and that the hero’s legend and the town’s future are decided by the same act.

Tap this panel to pause the scroll. Tap again to resume — exactly as in the game.

The readied-move bonuses — one standard set

Readying a move from its tile carries a bonus into the hero’s next battle. For forged heroes, the pattern is one standard set:

  • First move — +2 to hit / +2 damage on the first attack
  • Second move — +1 to all attacks
  • Third move — the first counter against you is reduced by 4

Replays, credits & your credits tune

A hero’s story does not end when a run ends. Every finished session run a character played in is saved to that character’s history, and the character sheet carries a video viewer built into the side of the sheet — so the sheet holds not just who the hero is, but the endings they were part of, ready to watch again from the sheet itself.

The sheet

Portraits, Look, moves, and tales stay where they have always been — the viewer does not replace the sheet. It sits alongside it, in the sheet’s side panel, waiting for a run to be chosen.

Every run this character played in is listed in the viewer’s drop-down history — the character’s own session runs, and no one else’s.

Click a run — watch its ending

Clicking any saved run in the drop-down plays that run in the viewer: the end game and replay highlight reel, rolling into the run’s scrolling credits, with music underneath. Each entry is that run’s own ending — pick a different run from the history and the viewer plays that one instead.

The tune shelf lives in the Forge — not in the sheet

The music comes from a shelf of 10 YouTube song videos held in the Forge app itself. Those 10 links are not stored inside each character sheet — the sheets carry the viewer and the run history, while the Forge holds the shelf, once, for everyone.

From that shelf, you pick any one of the 10 and set it as your “default credits tune.” From then on, whenever you choose a session run replay from a sheet’s drop-down, the viewer loads your default tune’s YouTube video as the music for that replay — no re-picking, run after run. Change your mind in the Forge, and the next replay uses the new default.

How the music plays — loop, then finish out

A song is usually shorter than a credits roll, so the default tune does not simply play once and stop. It follows one fixed rule:

While credits roll The tune loops The default YouTube video plays on loop, repeating as many times as the scrolling credits need.
→
When credits stop Looping switches off The moment the credits finish rolling, the viewer stops looping — it does not start the song over again.
→
To the end Finish out, then stop The current play of the video/song plays through to its own end — and then playback stops. No more looping.

From the DM screen — the campfire push

The digital game is not the only door a run comes through. A tabletop session is run from the DM screen version — the DM Guide — and the campfire sessions played that way earn their credits the same way a digital run does. From that DM screen, the DM can push the session’s credits — ending included — into each band member’s own character sheet in their Forge, so a campfire night lands in the same sheet history, and the same viewer drop-down, as a run played in the game itself.

A campfire session runs offline, away from any connection — so the push does not have to happen at the table, or even that night. It can happen any time after the session, once the DM is back online: the credits wait with the DM until there is a connection, and then they go out to every band member’s sheet.

That holds for any table sitting, not just the campfire. The DM runs the screen version on a laptop, and players who do not have the screen version can go fully old school — just printed character sheets and dice: the DM prints the character sheets for them to play from. After the session run, the DM can still push the credits to every character sheet where the character lives — in its user’s Forge, in their Stable of 20. Paper was the instrument for the night; the Forge sheet is the character’s permanent home, and that is where the record lands.

→
Any time after · back online The DM pushes the credits Once the DM is back online — that night or later — the DM pushes the session’s credits from the DM screen. There is no deadline on the push.
→
In each member’s Forge The run joins the sheet The campfire run arrives in each band member’s own character sheet history, ready to pick from the viewer drop-down and replay like any other saved run.

Where this stands

This replay system — the DM screen’s campfire push included — is designed and documented, and it arrives with the Forge’s opening and the game’s next release — the same Firebase setup sitting described below. It is not live in the public doors today, and the 10 song links are not named on this page — they will be the Forge’s shelf once they are supplied and set.

Where it stands — honestly

The Forge itself is built. It opens to the group once its one-time Firebase setup sitting is done, and game import rides with the game’s next release — the public doors still serve the previous edition of the game today. The replays, the credits tune, and the DM screen’s campfire push above ride that same opening and release; they are designed, not yet live.

Nothing on this page is a promise of a date.
